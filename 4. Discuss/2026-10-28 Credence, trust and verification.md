Gemini's figures, cases and references are unchecked unless they link to Zotero.

## Question explored
How can buyers trust qualities they cannot check, and which verification model suits smallholders?
- Quoc's question: What is the "lemons" problem?
- **Q8.** When environmental and social attributes are credence goods, how can digital transparency and traceability (blockchain, lightweight QR) reduce consumer scepticism without creating prohibitive compliance and digital-literacy barriers for smallholders?
- **Q9.** Can single-commodity certification ever secure household livelihoods, or does resilience require diversified farming and local processing?
- **Q10.** How can verification move beyond expensive third-party audits to low-cost credibility that keeps international market access?

## Main points
**The lemons problem (Gemini, after Akerlof, 1970).** When sellers know a product's quality and buyers do not, buyers will only pay an average price. Sellers of good products then leave, and only "lemons" remain. In sustainable food, safety and chemical-free production are credence attributes. Without credible verification, unverified claims flood the market, consumers refuse to pay a premium, good producers exit and trust collapses. Gemini linked this to Thailand's organic promotion (see [[2026-10-28 Consumers, prices and nudges]]; the story is unsourced).

**Q8, transparency and feedback (Gemini).**
- High-tech transparency (blockchain, heavy QR systems) often backfires in smallholder chains: it adds cost and demands digital literacy.
- What builds trust is credible two-way feedback: PGS, cooperative-led traceability and direct links between buyers, including institutional buyers, and farming communities.
- Gemini also cited a meta-analysis (Tran et al., 2024): consumers pay about 32% more for traceable food, but hiding the information behind a QR code cuts the premium by 17% compared with showing it directly.

**Q9, commodity certification against diversification (Gemini).** Schemes for single crops (e.g. Rainforest Alliance cocoa, export cashew) may raise that crop's price or yield. But households stay exposed to crashes, pests and climate shocks. Diversified systems act as biological and economic insurance: they smooth income, protect soil and improve diets. Gemini's example was rice–fish–vegetable systems in the IP1+ pilots in Battambang and Kampong Thom.

**Q10, the PGS dilemma (Gemini).** PGS work well for domestic and territorial markets because they rest on peer accountability and avoid audit fees. EU and North American import rules do not recognise community peer review. Gemini's "nested" or dual-track answer:
- PGS for local markets and public procurement;
- group certification, or bridging PGS data to formal equivalents (organic, CamGAP, ASEAN GAP), only when aggregating volume for export.

## Where I landed
*My own conclusions.*
- Q8: The concept of credence attributes (like trust?) is quite clear. The key is transparency and feedback, which help build credence.
- Q9: Single-commodity certification can deliver, but we should not prioritise it. Diversification is better for economic risk mitigation, ecological conservation, climate resilience and nutrition.
- Q10: This is a dilemma. PGS is commonly known, but I don't think it is internationally recognised as a gold standard. In many cases, PGS needs to incorporate other standards that enable exports.

## Open questions
- *Claude's note on "credence attributes (like trust?)":* the catalogue pages keep the two apart. [[Credence attributes]] are a property of the product: qualities buyers cannot verify. [[Trust in food chains]] is the buyer's response to that uncertainty. Transparency and feedback work on trust, which is why they help sell credence goods.
- Is there evidence that PGS can be bridged to export certification? Zotero has PGS studies from Viet Nam (Grovermann et al., 2024; Tuyet & Whitney, 2018), Bolivia (Jacobi et al., 2023) and a cost comparison (Kaufmann et al., 2023).
- Check Gemini's figures from Tran et al. (2024): the 32% premium and the 17% reduction for QR codes.
