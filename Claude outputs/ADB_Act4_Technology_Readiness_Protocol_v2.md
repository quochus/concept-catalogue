## 1. Technical approach and methodology

### 1.1 Background

Son La's fruit and vegetable supply chains face post-harvest losses, food safety and quality inconsistencies, fragmented aggregation, and limited access to demanding market channels in Hanoi, Bac Ninh and Hai Phong. A logistics center, cold chain and storage systems could connect Son La's production zones to these markets. Whether it can function depends partly on whether the technologies it needs, and the actors who would use and run them, are ready.

The assessment is one of five interconnected activities (market demand, investment environment, location suitability, technology readiness, institutional arrangements). Activity 4 draws on market demand findings (Activity 1) and feeds into institutional arrangements (Activity 5) and the Consolidated Feasibility Report (Activity 6).

### 1.2 Objective

Assess current technological practices in Son La's fruit and vegetable supply chain and the readiness and capacities of key actors to adopt and sustain logistics-related technology solutions for the operation of a logistics center, cold chain and storage systems.

### 1.3 Key concepts

- **Technology readiness** covers two things: how mature and proven a technology is for use, as measured by technology readiness levels (Mankins, 2009), and how able and willing its intended users and the surrounding system are to adopt and sustain it (Parasuraman, 2000; Sartas et al., 2020). This assessment focuses mainly on the second, the readiness and capacities of supply chain actors.
- **Logistics-related technologies** are the physical and digital tools used between harvest and the consumer to keep produce in good condition, monitor its condition, and coordinate and trace its movement (adapted from Kitinoja et al., 2011; Kaur & Watson, 2024).
- **Post-harvest handling and storage** is the set of practices, facilities and technologies used from harvest until sale or processing (harvesting, sorting and grading, packing, pre-cooling, storage and transport) to keep produce marketable and limit physical and economic losses (Affognon et al., 2015; Kaur & Watson, 2024).
- **Cold chain** is the linked set of pre-cooling, cold storage and temperature-controlled transport, connected by temperature monitoring, that keeps perishable produce within a product-specific temperature range from harvest to consumer (Makule et al., 2022; Mercier et al., 2017).
- **Traceability** is the ability to identify a product or lot and to access recorded information about its origin, transformations and attributes, backward to its sources and forward to its buyers (Olsen & Borit, 2018).

### 1.4 Desk review

The team will review existing assessments of relevant technology adoption in Vietnam's Northern highland supply chains. Technical requirements, covering cold storage systems, temperature monitoring, traceability, and safety and quality management, will be reviewed with consideration of the expected scale and commodity profile of a Son La logistics center, cold chain and storage systems.

Existing evidence points to the gaps the review will examine. In Vietnam, more than 90% of agricultural products transported to agricultural wholesale markets move without cold storage or refrigerated transport, and shippers treat the cold chain as an expense rather than an added value (Ho et al., 2022, p. 48). In Moc Chau, Son La, farmer groups used QR codes to trace the origin of vegetables supplied to retailers, but because QR codes add cost, they used them only for retailers that required them (Sen, 2017, pp. 101, 103).

### 1.5 Key informant interviews (8–10 KIIs)

- *Son La supply-side actors (cooperatives, traders, logistics operators and agribusinesses)* with experiences regarding cold storage, quality control, or traceability systems: current technology practices, operational experience, maintenance challenges, financial capacity for technology investment, capacity utilization, energy reliability, data exchange, and willingness to use and pay for logistics center services.
- *Demand-side actors (wholesalers, modern retailers, HoReCa buyers and institutional procurement)*: cold chain, post-harvest handling, packaging and traceability requirements; expected logistics center services; reliability standards; and willingness to pay for services.
- *Son La DAE extension services and relevant technical departments*: existing programs supporting technology adoption, identified gaps and constraints.
- *Technology solution providers with experience in comparable fresh fruit and vegetable supply chain contexts in Vietnam*: available cold storage, monitoring, and traceability solutions, costs, maintenance requirements, and track record in similar applications (remote interviews if applicable).
- *Development programs with technology components active in Son La or northern Vietnam*: lessons from technology adoption interventions, common challenges and risks, and conditions for sustainable adoption (remote interviews if applicable).

### 1.6 Analysis

Findings will produce:

- a technology landscape map across cold storage, monitoring, traceability, and quality control;
- a cost comparison of the technologies to produce a prioritized list of technologies;
- a gap analysis against the technology requirements of a logistics center, cold chain and storage systems;
- an assessment of adoption capacity constraints by actor type; and
- prioritized technology investment needs.

### 1.7 Outputs

- **O4.1** Inception report
- **O4.2** Progress report
- **O4.3** Technical report on technology readiness assessment covering current practices, identified gaps, capacity constraints, and prioritized technology investment recommendations. Findings feed into Activity 5 and the Consolidated Feasibility Report.

## 2. Study participants and data collection tools

Participants are adults (18+) in professional roles. KII tools are organized by respondent group, with parts for each activity, so that no one is interviewed more than once. Activity 4 questions are asked in the Activity 4 part of each group tool (Annex); interviewers skip parts that do not apply to a respondent. Each question is followed by suggested probes in brackets; interviewers may ask neutral clarifying follow-ups within the topic but will not introduce new topics. Tools are prepared in English and Vietnamese.

## 3. Data management

Data management follows the Research Protocol (v4):

- Interview data (audio, notes, transcripts) and desk review extracts are stored on a secure, password-protected server with access restricted to the research team, using the file naming convention YYYY-MM-DD_activitiesName_Source.
- Each participant is given a code; names and contact details are kept in a separate file. Audio recordings are deleted once transcripts are checked.
- For the desk review, a screenshot or archived copy of each source is saved with the extraction record.
- Coding is agreed in regular team meetings, and a coding decision log is kept.
- Data are retained for at least five years after study completion.

## 4. Ethical considerations

Ethical procedures follow the Research Protocol (v4):

- Informed consent is obtained before each interview: written (signature or thumbprint) in person, verbal for remote interviews. The consent statement explains that the study is a feasibility assessment, that no investment decision has been made, and that participation carries no benefit, obligation or advantage in any future selection or contract. Interviews are recorded only with permission.
- Participation is voluntary; participants may skip any question or withdraw at any time without consequence.
- Business information (volumes, capacities, costs, prices, fees) is reported only in aggregate or as ranges and never linked to a named business. This applies equally to cost and price information from technology solution providers.
- Findings from government interviews are reported by agency type or in aggregate; quotes are used only with permission and without identifying titles.

# ANNEX. Activity 4 questionnaires

*Reproduced from the Data Collection Tools (v2). Ask after Parts I (interviewee) and II (organization) of each group tool.*

## Tool 1. Supply-side actors – (iv) Technology readiness (Act 4)

- What facilities and equipment do you currently have for post-harvest handling, storage and transport, and for which commodities? *(Probe: pre-cooling, cold storage, dry storage, refrigerated vehicles, sorting/grading and packing equipment, temperature/humidity monitoring, quality testing, traceability (QR codes, records, software))*
- For each major facility or item of equipment: when was it installed or purchased, who owns and operates it, is it currently operational, and what is its capacity? *(Probe: own staff, cooperative members, contracted service provider, buyer; tonnes per day or per batch, tonnes of storage capacity, number of vehicles)*
- How intensively are these facilities used in peak and low seasons? Is capacity sufficient for your current needs? If not, when and where are the main shortages? Are there periods when facilities are unused or underused? Why? *(Probe: cost, seasonality, technical problems, location, insufficient demand; this helps distinguish a genuine capacity gap from underuse of existing capacity)*
- What temperature, humidity, storage duration or other technical conditions do your main commodities require? Are these set by buyers, standards/certification schemes, or your own practice? Are the standards buyers require appropriate to your production and business conditions? Who inspects compliance?
- Do you have standard procedures for operating cold storage, refrigerated transport, temperature monitoring and traceability? How are temperature, humidity and other conditions monitored and recorded, and how are the records used to manage quality, storage and delivery?
- Which important technologies or equipment do you currently lack? What are the main barriers to adopting or expanding them? If you do not use traceability, why not? *(Probe: investment cost, operating cost, electricity, lack of demand, technical skills, maintenance/service availability, unsuitable technology, insufficient volume, lack of space)*
- Which technology gaps cause the greatest problems for product quality, losses, shelf-life or access to higher-value markets?
- What problems have you experienced with equipment operation, maintenance or reliability? How easy is it to obtain maintenance services, spare parts and technical support locally? *(Probe: breakdowns, spare parts, technical support, calibration)*
- How significant are electricity availability and energy costs for operating cold-chain equipment? Have power interruptions caused product losses or equipment problems? How are these managed?
- Do you exchange product, quality, traceability or logistics information electronically with buyers, processors or other partners? Which systems/platforms are used, and can they share information with partners' systems? What information would you need to receive from, or provide to, a logistics center?
- Do staff have the skills to operate and maintain the technologies currently in use? What skills are lacking, and what training or technical support (including from equipment suppliers) would be needed for new or more advanced systems?
- What technologies or technical services would you expect a logistics center in Son La to provide? Which would be essential and which desirable but not essential? *(Probe: pre-cooling, cold storage, dry storage, refrigerated transport, sorting/grading, packing, temperature/humidity monitoring, quality testing, traceability)*
- What technical conditions would the center need to meet for you to use its services? Would you prefer an integrated service (from receiving/collection through pre-cooling, storage, packing, transport and delivery) or individual services? Why? *(Probe: temperature control, reliability, food safety/hygiene, monitoring, data/traceability, operating hours, turnaround time)*
- Would you be willing to pay for services such as cold storage, pre-cooling, refrigerated transport, temperature monitoring or traceability? Which services, on what pricing basis, and at approximately what fee? *(Probe: VND/kg, VND/tonne, VND/tonne/day, per trip, monthly service fee)*

## Tool 2. Demand-side actors – (ii) Technology readiness (Act 4)

- What cold-chain requirements apply to the main products you buy from Son La?
  - Required temperature range during storage and transport
  - Maximum time from harvest to pre-cooling, and from harvest to delivery
  - Required storage duration and shelf-life on arrival
  - Need for refrigerated transport; temperature monitoring and recording requirements
- Are these requirements set by your company, downstream buyers, or food-safety/certification standards? What happens when the required temperature or delivery time is not maintained?
- What post-harvest handling, packaging formats, materials, sizes or labelling do you require before produce reaches you? *(Probe: sorting/grading, cleaning, packing, pre-cooling, cold storage)*
- What traceability and documentation do you require from Son La suppliers? *(Probe: QR codes, growing-area/production records, batch/lot identification, food-safety and pesticide residue test results, harvest date, temperature records, digital records)*
- Which traceability or digital systems/platforms do you use? Are they compatible with suppliers' systems? What information must be available electronically, and at what point in the supply chain?
- Which technical or logistics capabilities are missing or inadequate in the Son La supply chain? Which gaps most limit your ability to buy more from Son La? *(Probe: pre-cooling, cold storage, refrigerated transport, sorting/grading, packaging, quality testing, traceability, temperature monitoring)*
- Which services would you expect a Son La logistics center to provide? Which are essential and which desirable? Would you prefer an integrated "one-stop" service or individual services selected to your needs? Why? *(Probe: collection/aggregation, sorting and grading, packing/repacking, pre-cooling, cold storage, dry/ambient storage, temperature/humidity monitoring, quality/safety testing, traceability and digital documentation, refrigerated transport, dispatch/consolidation for lowland delivery)*
- What level of reliability would you require from the logistics center, and what would the consequences be if it failed to maintain the required temperature, storage conditions or delivery schedule? *(Probe: temperature consistency, equipment uptime, turnaround time, delivery punctuality, data availability, emergency handling)*
- Would you be willing to pay for additional logistics/cold-chain services if they improved quality, shelf-life or reliability? Which services, on what pricing basis, and at approximately what fee? *(Probe: VND/kg, VND/tonne, VND/tonne/day, per trip, service package)*
- How would your technical or logistics requirements change if your purchases from Son La increase? Which new technologies or services do you expect to need in the next 3–5 years?

## Tool 3. Government officers and extension agents – (iv) Technology readiness (Act 4)

- Which government or development programs have supported cold storage, post-harvest handling, quality control, temperature management or traceability in Son La? What technologies or facilities were introduced, where, and what were the main results and challenges?
- What cold storage, pre-cooling, refrigerated transport and other post-harvest infrastructure currently exists in Son La? Are existing facilities fully used, seasonally underused or concentrated in particular areas? Where are the main gaps in capacity or geographic coverage?
- How widely are cold storage, pre-cooling, refrigerated transport, temperature monitoring and traceability currently used by cooperatives and enterprises?
- Which traceability systems, platforms or tools are used in Son La, and for which value chains and market destinations? Are they connected or interoperable with systems used by buyers, processors or other provinces?
- Why have some cooperatives and enterprises not implemented traceability? *(Probe: investment cost, operating cost, technical skills, lack of buyer demand, system complexity, connectivity, maintenance)*
- What lessons have been learned from projects introducing cold-chain, post-harvest, quality-control or traceability technologies in Son La? Which have been adopted and sustained, and which have faced difficulties? Why?
- What is the current status of VietGAP, growing-area codes and other certifications in the main fruit and vegetable chains? What are the main difficulties in maintaining them, and are current standards appropriate to local production conditions? Who is responsible for inspection and verification?
- Does the agency use digital tools or databases to manage growing-area codes, certifications or traceability? What information is captured, who maintains it, can it be shared electronically with other agencies or supply-chain actors, and what are the main limitations?
- What are the main technical and operational challenges affecting cold-chain and post-harvest facilities in Son La? *(Probe: electricity reliability, energy costs, equipment maintenance, spare parts, technical skills, availability of local technical support)*
- Which technologies or technical services will be most needed to support Son La's fruit and vegetable market development over the next 3–5 years? Which gaps could be addressed through a shared logistics center?
- Are there existing or planned logistics, cold-chain or post-harvest facilities in Son La that could complement, compete with or connect to a future logistics center?

## Tool 5. Development agencies – (ii) Technology readiness (Act 4)

- Which technologies or technical solutions has the program supported in Son La or comparable provinces (pre-cooling, cold storage, refrigerated transport, temperature monitoring, post-harvest handling, quality control, traceability)? What were the main results, and are the technologies still in use?
- What are the main challenges and risks affecting adoption and continued use of these technologies by cooperatives and enterprises? *(Probe: investment cost, operating cost, electricity, technical skills, maintenance, insufficient utilization/demand, technology suitability, market requirements)*
- What conditions are necessary for sustainable adoption and operation of these technologies? What cost-sharing or financing arrangements have worked in practice? *(Probe: operator training, maintenance and technical support, financing, reliable electricity, sufficient utilization, market incentives)*
- Where equipment or infrastructure was provided, what happened after project support ended? Who now operates, maintains and replaces it, how intensively is it used, and what are the main ongoing costs or problems?
- What cold-chain, post-harvest, quality-control or traceability interventions have other domestic or international organizations implemented in Son La? What worked well, what were the main limitations, and what gaps remain?
- Are there examples of shared logistics, cold-chain or post-harvest facilities supported by development programs in Son La or other northern mountainous provinces? What determined whether they were used and sustained?
- What are the most important technology or infrastructure gaps in Son La's fruit and vegetable value chains, and which could be addressed through a shared logistics center?

## Tool 6. Technology solution providers – (i) Technology readiness (Act 4)

- Which technology solutions would be technically and operationally suitable for a potential logistics center in Son La? What capacity or scale ranges would you recommend for each function?
  - Pre-cooling
  - Cold rooms/cold storage
  - Refrigerated transport
  - Temperature and humidity monitoring (sensors/IoT)
  - Traceability software
- What key technical specifications and operating conditions should guide technology selection? Can the systems integrate with other equipment, logistics-management systems or traceability platforms? *(Probe: temperature/humidity ranges, cooling time, storage duration, throughput, monitoring frequency, data storage)*
- What are the indicative investment and operating costs of different options, including electricity/energy, labor, maintenance and consumables? What is the expected useful life, and what major replacement costs should be anticipated? What causes costs to vary? *(Probe: capacity, temperature range, automation, energy efficiency, site conditions)*
- What are the typical energy requirements of the proposed systems? What energy-efficiency measures and backup-power solutions would you recommend for a facility in Son La?
- What business models are available? What are the typical contract periods, payment arrangements and responsibilities for operation, maintenance and replacement under each? *(Probe: direct purchase, lease, equipment-as-a-service, pay-per-use)*
- What maintenance and after-sales services are available in the northern mountainous provinces? Where are technical teams and spare parts located? What is the typical breakdown response time, what preventive maintenance is required, and what uptime should be expected?
- Have you implemented similar cold-chain, storage, logistics or digital technology projects in mountainous or northern provinces? What were the results, operational challenges, common failures and lessons learned? Can you point to comparable facilities that are operating successfully?
- What local conditions in Son La could affect performance or operating costs, and how can these risks be mitigated? *(Probe: electricity reliability, terrain and road access, climate, water availability, connectivity, availability of skilled operators)*
- Can the technology be installed in modular stages and expanded as demand grows? What are the minimum viable capacities and practical expansion limits?
