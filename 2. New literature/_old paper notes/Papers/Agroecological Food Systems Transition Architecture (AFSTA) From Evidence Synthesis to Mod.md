## Takeaways

- Structured narrative synthesis across six domains: climate hazards, production responses, trade/network transmission, cross-border risk, transition pathways and outcomes.
- Africa focus with comparative settings in Kenya, Mozambique and Senegal.
- Argues trade-network structure is part of the system state: networks can buffer or transmit climate shocks.
- Transition pathways reconfigure the system and thus how future shocks propagate; outcomes should be assessed separately to expose trade-offs.
- A conceptual architecture only — no calibration or model results.

## Why it matters

Its hazard → production → trade-transmission chain is a useful scaffold for framing El Niño 2026–27 impact pathways (incl. cross-border/trade effects) in the El Niño food and nutrition security concept note.

## Abstract

Proposes AFSTA, an evidence-based architecture linking transboundary climate risk transmission via food trade with agroecological transition pathways in Africa, derived from a structured narrative synthesis; defines system boundaries and modelling requirements but is not an executable model.
