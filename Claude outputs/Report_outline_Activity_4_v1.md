## 1. Introduction and objectives

This activity will assess the adoption and readiness of logistics-related technologies relevant to the development of a logistics center, cold chain and storage systems in Son La, with a primary focus on private sector actors (ToR, para. 5). It reviews existing technological practices and tools in cold storage, monitoring, traceability and quality control. It examines the technical and financial capacity of supply chain actors to adopt and sustain technology solutions. It identifies key technology gaps and investment needs for logistics and cold chain development, and highlights opportunities for private sector participation in technology deployment and operation of the logistics center (ToR, para. 5).

**Objective** (Protocol v4): Assess current technological practices in Son La's fruit and vegetable supply chain and the readiness and capacities of key actors to adopt and sustain logistics-related technology solutions for the operation of a logistics center, cold chain and storage systems.

Activities include (ToR, Agri-food Systems and Food Supply Chain Specialist tasks):

- Review existing logistics-related technological practices and tools in cold storage, monitoring, traceability and quality control.
- Examine the technical and financial capacity of supply chain actors to adopt and sustain technology solutions, and identify technology gaps in support of a logistics center.

**Key concepts [C1].** *Technology readiness* is used as in the ToR: it covers both whether the technologies themselves are mature and proven for use, and the "technical and financial capacity of market actors to adopt and sustain technology solutions" (ToR, para. 1). *Technology appropriateness* is whether a technology that works is suited to Son La's places, crops, scale and users. Full definitions are in the Activity 4 protocol v2, section 1.3. Consumers' attitudes to technology are not assessed.

## 2. Methodology

The methods are desk review and key informant interviews (KIIs) (ToR, para. 5; Protocol v4).

### 2.1 Desk review

- **Technology adoption:** existing assessments of relevant technology adoption in Vietnam's northern highland supply chains (Protocol v4).
- **Technical requirements:** cold storage systems, temperature monitoring, traceability, and safety and quality management, reviewed against the expected scale and commodity profile of a Son La logistics center, cold chain and storage systems (Protocol v4).
- **Existing evidence in Vietnam and Son La [C2]:** for example, more than 90% of agricultural products transported to wholesale markets move without cold storage or refrigerated transport (Ho et al., 2022, p. 48); in Moc Chau, QR-code traceability was used only for retailers that required it, because of its cost (Sen, 2017, pp. 101, 103).
- **Programs and lessons [C3]:** reports of government and development programs that introduced cold-chain, post-harvest, quality-control or traceability technologies in Son La or other northern mountainous provinces. Protocol v4 does not list this source, but it partly replaces information otherwise asked of government and development agencies.
- **Regulations and standards [C3]:** national standards and regulations on cold storage, food safety, traceability and growing-area codes, each checked on vbpl.vn or thuvienphapluat.vn.

### 2.2 Key informant interviews (8–10 KIIs)

The primary respondents are private sector actors, as the ToR specifies. Government (including extension) and development agencies are secondary sources; where they have supplied equipment, training or a platform, they are also treated as technology providers **[C4]**.

- *Son La supply-side actors (cooperatives, traders, logistics operators and agribusinesses)* with experience of cold storage, quality control or traceability: current technology practices, operational experience, maintenance challenges, financial capacity for technology investment, capacity utilization, energy reliability, data exchange, and willingness to use and pay for logistics center services (Protocol v4).
- *Technology solution providers* with experience in comparable fresh fruit and vegetable supply chains in Vietnam: available cold storage, monitoring and traceability solutions, costs, maintenance requirements, and track record in similar applications (remote if needed) (Protocol v4).
- *Demand-side actors (wholesalers, modern retailers, HoReCa buyers, institutional procurement)*: cold chain, post-harvest handling, packaging and traceability requirements; expected logistics center services; reliability standards; and willingness to pay for services (Protocol v4). Most of this is already collected under Activity 1 (Section 3.2) **[C5]**.
- *Son La DAE extension services and relevant technical departments* (secondary): existing programs supporting technology adoption, identified gaps and constraints (Protocol v4).
- *Development programs with technology components in Son La or northern Vietnam* (secondary): lessons from technology adoption interventions, common challenges and risks, and conditions for sustainable adoption (remote if needed) (Protocol v4).

## 4. Analysis

Findings will produce (Protocol v4):

- a technology landscape map across cold storage, monitoring, traceability and quality control;
- a cost comparison of the technologies to produce a prioritized list of technologies;
- a gap analysis against the technology requirements of a logistics center, cold chain and storage systems;
- an assessment of adoption capacity constraints by actor type;
- prioritized technology investment needs;
- opportunities for private sector participation in technology deployment and operation of the logistics center **[C8]**.
