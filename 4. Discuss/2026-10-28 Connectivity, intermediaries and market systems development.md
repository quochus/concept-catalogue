## Question explored
How do intermediaries, connectivity and market systems development link values to working markets?
- **Q24 (reframed by Quoc).** How can intermediaries (collectors, traders, aggregators) be incentivised and transformed from extractive actors into value-adding partners in regenerative food systems? It replaced a question on cold chains and post-harvest technology.
- **Q26 (added by Quoc).** What is connectivity in sustainable food systems, why should it be strengthened, and how? Link it with values and market development.
- **Q27 (added by Quoc).** How can market systems development (MSD) be applied to change market functions without pushing smallholders into debt, risk or marginalisation by corporations?

## Main points
**Q24, intermediaries (Gemini).** Traders are often cast as extractive middlemen. But in LMICs they pay cash on delivery, carry transport risk and give informal emergency credit. Gemini's suggestions:
- **Co-aggregation:** bring traders into cooperative first-mile hubs and pay them a fair handling fee based on quality and compliance (residue-free, VietGAP or PGS produce), rather than squeezing farm-gate prices.
- **Traceability:** give collectors simple logging tools so their networks become the record of origin.

**Q26, what connectivity is (Gemini, citing "HLPE reports; agroecological connectivity frameworks").** Gemini defined it as the density, quality and multi-directional flow of information, resources, trust and power, in three dimensions:
- **Vertical:** along the chain, from producers through aggregators, processors and retailers to consumers.
- **Horizontal:** among peers: smallholders, communities, cooperatives, PGS peer review.
- **Territorial or diagonal:** between rural production and urban consumption, including local government, civil society, research and public institutions.

Why strengthen it: weak connectivity leaves farmers as price-takers with no market view, leaves consumers facing the lemons problem, and leaves policy cut off from farm realities.

How (Gemini's chain: shared values → connectivity bridge → market development): trust, safety and ecological care cannot be verified by paperwork alone, so connectivity carries them. Examples:
- PGS;
- direct cooperative storefronts on Zalo or TikTok, where consumers see the farm and farmers learn urban demand;
- wet markets (*chợ*) upgraded into "safe food nodes" linked to rural cooperatives, building on the norm of *thuận mua vừa bán*.

**Q27, MSD (Gemini).** MSD fixes the underlying reasons a market fails the poor, rather than handing out inputs or buying produce directly. It works on supporting functions (finance, information, logistics, technology) and on rules (standards, land policy, procurement), so that actors keep trading on commercial terms. Applied to RA:
- **Supporting functions:** youth-run bio-fertiliser and bio-pesticide businesses; transition credit through cooperative guarantee funds and vouchers rather than individual microloans; local extension.
- **Rules:** legal recognition of PGS and harmonised CamGAP/VietGAP; procurement anchors that bring in private midstream investment.
- **Intermediaries:** certified aggregation partners who earn service fees.

## Open questions
- *Claude's note:* Gemini's vertical, horizontal and territorial split differs from [[Connectivity]]. That page sets the narrow HLPE principle (proximity and confidence between producers and consumers) against a broad sustainability-science use, and groups the broad use into four dimensions, including human–nature and ecological links. Gemini's split leaves out the human–nature side and cites no source. Is it worth adding as a way to cut across actors?
- MSD works through commercial incentives. How does it handle the power questions in [[2026-10-28 Power, co-optation and the politics of RA]]? Compare [[Market systems development]] and [[Inclusive value chains]].
