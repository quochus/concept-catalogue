## Takeaways

- Conceptual review (not systematic), drawing on authors' experience in Kisumu, Kenya, and the literature.
- Formal features (licensing, taxation, designated space) aid traceability and enforcement but do not guarantee food safety.
- Informal vendors provide affordable, convenient food, flexible jobs, local supply and trust-based credit, with hygiene, waste and labour-protection downsides.
- Argues urban food systems work better with both sectors; recommends facilitative rather than punitive enforcement and reviewing fees that deter formalisation.

## Why it matters

Supports a 'facilitate, don't penalise' framing for engaging small vendors in the Son La SME nutrition training and for the informal-actor discussion in the NSAF Vietnam policy brief.

## Abstract

Reviews the characteristics, advantages and drawbacks of formality and informality in urban food markets, using Kisumu, Kenya as illustration, and argues that both contribute to urban food system sustainability while each carries negative effects.
