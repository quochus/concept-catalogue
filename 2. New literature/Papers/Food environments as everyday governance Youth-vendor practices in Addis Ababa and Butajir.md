## Takeaways

- Reframes food environments as relational configurations co-produced by youth and vendors, not fixed contexts that determine individual choice.
- Uses Social Practice Theories and Lived Experience Research: photo-journals, ethnographic observation and interviews in Addis Ababa and Butajira.
- Vendors enact informal governance through care, credit, shareable portions and daily negotiation of hygiene; youth balance socialising, nourishment and abstinence.
- Warns that standardised fixes (bans on street vending, replacing vendors) could destroy social infrastructure; suggests treating vendors and youth as co-governors and offering small infrastructure support.
- Exact AHV publication date not confirmed (earlier SSRN version); read from abstract plus authors' CIAT blog.

## Why it matters

Gives a theory-backed argument and method (practice theory, photo-journals) for working with small vendors as partners in the Son La SME nutrition training and KAP design, and for the informal side of the Foodscapes PoD.

## Abstract

Reconceptualizes food environments as dynamic, relational configurations co-produced through everyday interactions between youth consumers and food vendors in Addis Ababa and Butajira, Ethiopia, drawing on Social Practice Theories and Lived Experience Research. Informal governance by youth and vendors offers insights into how food system transformations can be pursued from the ground up.
