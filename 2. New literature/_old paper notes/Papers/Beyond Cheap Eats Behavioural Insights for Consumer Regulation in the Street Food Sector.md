## Takeaways

- PLS-SEM on a survey of 519 street-food consumers in Hanoi.
- Affordability, spatial convenience and cultural authenticity improve attitudes; perceived food-safety and urban environmental risks reduce them.
- Relational trust in vendors did not significantly shape attitudes — contrary to expectations.
- Concludes exclusionary enforcement won't cut demand; recommends graded hygiene certification, visible cleanliness cues and community-based vendor governance.

## Why it matters

Hanoi consumer evidence supporting a 'regulate-and-upgrade, don't evict' stance for vendors, usable in the Son La SME training rationale and the NSAF brief's informal-sector recommendations.

## Abstract

Survey of 519 Hanoi street-food consumers using PLS-SEM finds economic benefits, convenience and authenticity improve attitudes while food-safety and environmental risk perceptions reduce them; vendor trust is not significant. Recommends behaviourally informed regulation preserving spatial access while making hygiene visible and verifiable.
