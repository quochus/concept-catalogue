## Takeaways

- Uses a foodscapes lens plus participatory visual (photo-based) methods and reflexive thematic analysis with small-scale mangrove fishers in Indonesia, with explicit attention to gender.
- Three themes: the catch is bound up with the fisher's identity and skill; food places and practices are sources of wellbeing and joy; mangrove livelihoods depend on both land and sea, a link most visible in women's work.
- Argues global sustainability narratives frame mangroves/fisheries mainly by nutrition and ecosystem function, while fishers value food through socio-cultural meaning, reciprocity and place-based identity.
- Warns that interventions built on global narratives may misfit local realities; calls for food systems policy to weigh lived experience. Open access (CC BY).

## Why it matters

Directly usable as a Southeast Asian methodological and conceptual template for the Foodscapes PoD's Mekong Delta work on place-based meaning and local food heritage, including the land–water and gendered livelihood angle.

## Abstract

Mangroves and small-scale fisheries are often cited as important to sustainable food systems, yet dependent communities are marginalised in policy. Using a foodscapes lens and participatory visual methods, the study explores how Indonesian mangrove fishers connect food, people and place, finding that socio-cultural meaning, reciprocity and place identity matter as much as nutrition and environmental function.
