## Takeaways

- Links what households grow and eat, using Tanzania NPS 2020–21 plus FAOSTAT by agroecological zone.
- Methods: Simpson diversity, portfolio overlaps, Market Basket Analysis (support/confidence/lift) of co-consumed foods, and TOPSIS ranking of 19 crops on 10 indicators.
- Opportunity crops already sit in maize-based portfolios (maize–pigeon pea, maize–cowpea); legumes show more selective consumption links than staples.
- TOPSIS favours already-common crops (beans 1st, maize 2nd; millet 17th), a bias the authors acknowledge.
- Recommends linking diversification to nutrition programmes and public procurement, and matching public vs cash-flow finance to function.

## Why it matters

Market Basket Analysis and TOPSIS are transferable diet-centred diagnostic tools that could be showcased in the FSDS toolbox manuscript.

## Abstract

Policy brief proposing to use existing consumer baskets and producer portfolios to mainstream opportunity crops in Tanzania, combining household survey and FAOSTAT data with market basket analysis and TOPSIS crop ranking.
