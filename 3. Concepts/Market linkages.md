## Definitions
Market linkages became a development theme when liberalisation and supermarket growth left many smallholders unable to sell into new markets. Donors, NGOs and extension services acting as "linking organizations" began promoting the "linking farmers to markets" approach, "which usually involves organizing farmers into groups to supply identified markets" (Shepherd, 2007, PDF p. 7). The aim moved from one-off sales to approaches that "assume the development of long-term business relationships rather than support for ad hoc sales" (PDF p. 9).

Research then widened the focus from farmers to the relationship itself. In Vietnamese vegetable chains, Cadilhon et al. (2009, PDF p. 2) found hybrid forms of organisation and stressed "characterizing the type of business-to-business relationship" when trying to link farmers to markets.

**Working definition used here:** market linkages are durable business relationships between producers and buyers, built through collective organisation, intermediaries and enabling policies, that give producers reliable access to markets *(Claude's synthesis of Shepherd, 2007; Markelova et al., 2009)*.

## Features
**Mechanisms.** Collective action is the main route: groups help smallholders reduce entry barriers "by improving their bargaining power with buyers and intermediaries" (Markelova et al., 2009, p. 2). Standards and certification also open markets; in Vietnam, "Small firms particularly profit from the adoption of private international quality assurance programs" (Mergenthaler et al., 2008, p. 221).

**Conditions.** "Trust seems essential for the establishment and continuation of linkages" (Shepherd, 2007, PDF p. 10). Linkages also need flexibility, prior experience of collaboration, and an enabling environment set by government.

**Limits.** "Linkage activities presently reach only a small proportion of farmers" (Shepherd, 2007, PDF p. 12), and it "is not yet clear whether linkage projects are replicable and up-scalable" (PDF p. 11). Projects may also replace one group of suppliers with another rather than expanding the market.

## Implications
In Cambodia, "Producer cooperatives (PCs) could play a critical role in linking smallholder farmers to HVMs" (high-value markets) (Tray et al., 2021, p. 905). In northern Vietnam, a systems analysis named "market actor linkages and quality control" among the leverage points for safe vegetables (Ha et al., 2015, PDF p. 1). For IP1+, the concept frames how agroecological and regenerative producers in Laos and Cambodia might reach buyers who reward these practices; see [[Market systems development]] for the wider system view.
