## Takeaways

- Systematic review of 327 studies from 64 LMICs (screened from >23,000 records) on informal vendors in food environments; 84% of studies are urban.
- Fewer than a quarter of studies define informality explicitly; most use only the 'goods for market' ILO component, and only 10% consider vendors' social protection.
- Regulation, governance, gender, and access to water, sanitation and public space are the main sociopolitical factors shaping informal vending.
- Vendors' livelihood challenges centre on business operations, interactions and long working hours; authors argue policy will keep missing these actors without an interdisciplinary lens.

## Why it matters

Gives a ready evidence base and definitional framing for the informal-vendor component of the Son La SME nutrition training and the MSME finance PoD (e.g., justify KAP survey items on regulation, WASH and social protection).

## Abstract

Over one billion people in LMICs work in food systems, including informal traditional, street and mobile vendors. This systematic review of 327 studies across 64 countries examines how informality in food environments is defined and studied and how it intersects with nutrition, livelihoods and sociopolitical contexts, finding inconsistent definitions and highlighting regulation, governance, gender and WASH access as key factors.
