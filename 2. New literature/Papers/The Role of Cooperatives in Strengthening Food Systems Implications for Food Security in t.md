## Takeaways

- Case of Oriental Mindoro (high output, high food insecurity) using 2 FGDs, an online survey (53 members of 26 co-ops) and a validation workshop.
- Cooperatives operate across production, processing, distribution, consumption and waste; members rate their contribution to household food security at a mean 7.85/10 (perceived, not causal).
- Legal framework is supportive, but policy remains production-centric with little on marketing, consumption and waste.
- Constraints: low capitalisation, weak grant access, fragmented inter-cooperative ties, compliance burdens, dependency on government.
- Recommends policy coherence, financing windows for small co-ops, inter-cooperative alliances and better data.

## Why it matters

Philippine cooperative governance evidence that can inform stakeholder mapping for the Climate mobility PoD scaling to the Philippines and the cooperative component of Son La SME training.

## Abstract

Examines whether cooperatives, as collective-action institutions, strengthen food system governance in support of food security in Oriental Mindoro, Philippines; finds them active at all food system stages with perceived contributions to food security, constrained by limited capital, weak inter-cooperative collaboration and policy imbalances.
