## Takeaways

- Survey of 157 households in 11 peri-urban Hanoi wards; k-means clustering by purchase frequency and vendor-choice reasons.
- 'Frequent buyers' had slightly higher dietary diversity, but driven by oils, fats and sweets rather than nutrient-dense foods.
- Proximity, affordability and assortment drove vendor choice; distance-based access did not differ between clusters, though transport modes did.
- Argues behaviour-based segmentation complements income/location approaches, and supermarket expansion may accelerate processed-food consumption.

## Why it matters

Fresh Vietnam evidence that HDDS gains can mask processed-food intake — a useful caution for diet metrics in the diet-centred framework / FSDS toolbox and for the NSAF brief's food-environment section.

## Abstract

Cross-sectional survey of 157 peri-urban Hanoi households examining how purchasing patterns and vendor choice relate to household dietary diversity; frequent buyers had higher HDDS largely through processed food groups, and proximity, affordability and assortment dominated vendor choice.
