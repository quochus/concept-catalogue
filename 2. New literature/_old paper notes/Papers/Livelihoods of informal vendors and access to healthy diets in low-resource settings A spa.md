## Takeaways

- Random sample of 530 food vendors along an urban-rural gradient in Kenya, analysed with spatial lag, spatial error and combined spatial autoregressive models.
- Both vendors' personal backgrounds and the diversity/healthiness of foods they sell vary significantly across space.
- The combined model shows spatially structured links between vendor background and the healthiness and variety of their offer.
- Argues interventions for healthy food access should account for vendor-level livelihood histories, not just mapping outlets; proceedings paper, abstract-level detail only.

## Why it matters

Supports targeting by vendor profile in the Son La SME nutrition training and KAP baseline design, and adds a spatial-method idea for food vendor analysis.

## Abstract

Examines how informal food vendors' backgrounds relate to the diversity and quality of foods sold in Kenya, finding spatial heterogeneity in both and spatial associations between them, with implications for policies on healthy food access in LMICs.
