## Takeaways

- Uses Foucault's 'subjugated knowledges' to analyse how place-based, experiential knowledge is mobilised, recognised or suppressed in food system transformation.
- Two Swedish regional cases (Skåne and Värmland).
- Governance routines, regulatory norms and national policy agendas constrain the transformative potential of local knowledge.
- Local actors' agency nonetheless opens alternative pathways to systemic change; the paper maps strategies actors use to sustain marginalised knowledge.

## Abstract

The paper examines how power-knowledge relations shape the mobilisation, recognition and suppression of local knowledge in food system transformation, drawing on Foucault's subjugated knowledges and two Swedish regional cases. It argues governance routines, regulatory norms and national agendas constrain transformative potential while local agency creates alternative pathways.
