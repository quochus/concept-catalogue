## Question explored
What role do values play in scaling and transition, and why are they not enough on their own?
- Quoc's questions: How well supported is the "health halo"? How influential is the intrinsic–instrumental–relational typology, and how does it affect interventions? Are we reducing everything to economics in the end?
- **Q11.** Since consumers conflate sustainability with personal health, should interventions lead with food safety and nutrition, or try to build intrinsic motivation for ecological stewardship?
- **Q25.** When market premiums fail or fluctuate, which non-market mechanisms (agrarian movements, CSA, public stewardship payments) give the most stable basis for long-term restoration?

## Main points
**Values as entry points (Gemini).** Values give a normative compass and a shared vocabulary, but "values alone do not pay bills, build cold storage, change national budgets, or rewrite trade laws". Relying on values alone moralises a structural failure.
- In emerging markets, consumers act on values once they become personal benefits: safety, no residues, nutrition (the "health halo").
- For smallholders, values are often relational (soil, community independence, traditional knowledge). They give the cohesion needed to form cooperatives and alternative food networks.
- Gemini linked this to the "six-pillar added-value framework", the framework in Quoc's [[Values of sustainable agri-food products]].

**Why values are not enough (Gemini).**
- **The say–do gap.** If a sustainable product costs "30%" more, households with tight budgets buy conventional food.
- **Transition debt.** A farmer who values soil health cannot absorb a multi-season yield drop without finance, insurance or off-take.
- **Midstream power.** Even when producers and consumers share values, traders and processors capture the value.

What must come with values: legal and financial infrastructure (cooperative banking and procurement entities), transition support, and credible low-cost verification.

**The health halo (Gemini).** Gemini called it "extremely well-supported", citing Cook et al. (2023) and Majer et al. (2022). It said that leading with safety, residues and nutrition earns higher willingness to pay in emerging economies than leading with carbon or biodiversity (Cook et al., 2023; My et al., 2018).

**The value typology (Gemini).** The typology is influential through IPBES (Arias-Arévalo et al., 2017; Himes et al., 2024; Pascual et al.).
- **Instrumental:** worth as a means to human ends.
- **Intrinsic:** worth as an end in itself.
- **Relational:** worth arising from meaningful relationships between people and nature, or among people.

Interventions built only on instrumental value, such as cash for carbon, can produce "mercenary compliance" that stops when payments stop. Successful initiatives build on relational values: pride, peer trust, collective identity (De Lucio et al., 2025; Vizuete et al., 2025). Gemini recommended combining instrumental tools (credit, fair prices) with relational ones (cooperatives, cultural pride).

**Is it all economics? (Gemini).** No, but economics is the "gatekeeper". Identity, trust, culture and power matter. In Gemini's words, "Economics doesn't explain all motivations, but it determines whether motivations can be survived."

**Q25, non-market foundations (Gemini).**
- Public stewardship payments (PES) made regardless of food prices, treating soil restoration as a public good.
- Community-supported agriculture, where upfront subscriptions share risk with farmers.
- Agrarian solidarity and social movements that keep relational values and cohesion alive through market downturns.

## Where I landed
*My own conclusions.*
- Our discussion keeps touching the idea of values. Values are not enough: they are entry points or shared normative grounds, but more is needed to make scaling and transition succeed.
- Q11: Given that the health halo is well evidenced, we should promote both, but not push. Use the environmental aspects as complements to the focus on health values.
- Q25 is the question of values again. Building shared ground is critical, but we also need other things to make the connection material.
- Open for me: are we finally reducing everything to economic issues?
