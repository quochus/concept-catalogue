## Takeaways

- Opinion piece proposing the PHOFE framework: micro food enterprises (vendors, kiosks, stalls, food trucks) as public-health actors in urban food environments.
- Five dimensions — food safety/quality, nutrition, economic and spatial access, commercial sustainability, environmental responsibility — underpinned by governance.
- Frames a 'commercial–public health alignment problem': healthy offerings must be commercially viable, and small entrepreneurs should not carry sole responsibility.
- Proposes an enable–incentivise–assure governance model (registration, training, shared vending space; criteria-linked rewards; undiluted safety standards).
- Conceptual only, no empirical data; indicators proposed for future testing.

## Why it matters

The PHOFE dimensions and enable–incentivise–assure model could structure the Son La SME nutrition training curriculum and the KAP baseline indicators on business viability vs. nutrition.

## Abstract

Opinion article arguing small food businesses shape urban food environments and public health, proposing the Public Health-Oriented Food Entrepreneurship (PHOFE) framework linking enterprise viability with health goals across five dimensions underpinned by governance, and outlining enabling, incentivising and assurance functions for policy.
