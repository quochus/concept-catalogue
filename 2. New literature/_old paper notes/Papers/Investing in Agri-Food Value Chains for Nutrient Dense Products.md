## Takeaways

- SMEs move most food in LMICs but are underfinanced for nutrient-dense products; weak domestic demand for nutritious foods in turn constrains the credit market for these chains.
- Perishability, poor transport and uncertain demand deter investment in fruit, vegetable and animal-source food chains.
- Offers a framework of direct vs indirect (rental-market) investments at four value-chain levels, tested with formative KIIs and FGDs in Bangladesh maize–feed–dairy and oilseed corridors.
- Bangladesh dairy and meat lack cold storage, and machinery markets are skewed to grain; the maize-feed-dairy link connects staple and nutrient-dense chains.
- Recommends nutrition-focused impact investing and donor de-risking, rental markets for equipment, seasonal repayment/insurance products, and pairing supply investment with demand-side nutrition campaigns.

## Why it matters

Its investment-level framework and finance-product recommendations can directly structure the problem analysis and intervention menu of the MSME finance PoD, and its cold-storage gap evidence supports the Son La cold chain case.

## Abstract

Report (no formal abstract) arguing that targeted private investment in SMEs along value chains for nutrient-dense foods can improve diets, describing how limited demand and financing gaps keep these chains underdeveloped, with a Bangladesh case study.
