## Takeaways

- Food systems drive roughly 30% of human-caused GHG emissions and are the leading cause of transgressing several planetary boundaries (land, biosphere integrity, nitrogen, freshwater, novel entities).
- Shifting to plant-rich diets and cutting waste is framed as the main demand-side lever, creating 'option space' that makes supply-side climate targets feasible; demand-side measures yield co-benefits where single supply-side measures create trade-offs.
- Cites a global model in which a bundle of 23 measures, individually trade-off-laden, together avert ~182 million life-years lost per year by 2050, nearly halve nitrogen surpluses and offset poverty side-effects (~7% of GDP in welfare gains).
- Flags large agricultural job losses (India and China case boxes) and calls for just-transition finance, demand-side measures in NDCs, and stronger UNFCCC agriculture work; notes models underplay political economy (lobbying, electoral cycles, price resistance).

## Why it matters

Provides a citable 'bundling to manage trade-offs' argument and policy asks (demand-side measures in NDCs, procurement, dietary guidelines) for the APEC low-emission incentives concept note and the diet-centred framework manuscript.

## Abstract

A shift to plant-rich diets is presented as the primary demand-side lever for meeting climate and environmental goals while improving health; realising co-benefits and managing labour impacts requires bundled, cross-sectoral policies and a just transition. (Summary in own words.)
